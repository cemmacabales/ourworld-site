What we keep, and who sees it

tinsy is a little island for two people. What you put on it stays between the two of you.

The short version

  • No ads
  • No analytics
  • No tracking
  • Nothing sold
  • Nothing shared

We keep what the app needs to work: your sign-in, and the island you and your partner build. Only the two of you can see your island. You can delete your account, and everything that goes with it, from inside the app.

What we keep

  • Your sign-in. When you sign in with Apple, Apple gives us an ID for your account and an email address. If you chose to hide your email, it's Apple's private relay address, not your real one. Apple may also offer your name the first time you sign in; we don't keep it.
  • What you put on your island. The name you go by, the day it all started, the welcome note, your places and memories with their dates, the words on their stubs, your notes and ratings, your photos, and which memories you've already seen.
  • A copy on your phone. The app keeps a copy of your island on your device so it shows up quickly, even on a slow connection. Opening the app needs the internet. Signing out or deleting your account clears it.

We don't collect your location, contacts, or anything from other apps, and we don't use device identifiers for advertising.

Who can see it

  • The two of you. Every island has exactly two people on it, and only they can read or change it. There are no public profiles, no shared links, and no way for anyone else using tinsy to find or see your island.
  • The person who runs tinsy. We can reach the database to keep it working and to make backups. We don't look through your memories or photos, unless you ask us to help with something.

Where it's stored

Your island, sign-in and photos are stored with Supabase, on servers in Singapore, and travel there encrypted (HTTPS). Once a week we make a backup of the database and photos, kept privately on GitHub for 14 days and then deleted, so something lost by accident can be brought back.

Two other services are involved. Apple handles Sign in with Apple. Netlify hosts the app's own pages, fonts and pictures, so it sees the ordinary details of those requests, like your IP address. Your island and photos don't go through Netlify. Nothing is loaded from anyone else.

Deleting your account

  1. Tap your pass in the top corner, or the cottage, to open your season pass.
  2. Tap the pencil, then delete my account.
  3. Confirm, and Apple checks it's really you.

Your sign-in, notes, ratings and seen marks are deleted for good, and we ask Apple to revoke tinsy's access to your Apple ID. If your partner is still on the island, it stays with them, and the memories and photos you added stay too, with no name attached. If nobody else is on the island, the whole island goes with it: every memory, stub and photo. Backups that still hold your data are gone within 14 days.

Can't get into the app? Email us from the address on your account and we'll delete it for you.

Children

tinsy isn't made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly keep anything from them. If you think a child has signed up, let us know and we'll delete their account.

Changes

If what we keep or how we use it changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top. If it's a big change, the app will tell you too.
